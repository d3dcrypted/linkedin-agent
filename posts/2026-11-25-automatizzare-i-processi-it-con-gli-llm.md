I'll start with the boring part: I automated a process with LLMs.

The saving is real and shows up immediately. The cost of the error shows up later, spread over weeks, which is to say when I have already moved my attention somewhere else. In my case: never.

Here is the serious part, because it matters. A failing AI system can cost a market between 0.1% and 3.6% of annual revenue, and enterprise agentic AI sits at the top of that 3.6%. 70.1% of enterprise decision-makers have received plainly wrong answers at least once a month, and 13.6% lost money over it.

The bottleneck is checking the AI while it works, not making it work. For 51.2% a single problem takes at least five person-hours of investigating, verifying and fixing. Hours that were never in the project budget. By anyone. Me included.

The number that changed my method: workflows with a human gate were twice as likely to reach savings of 75% or more. The guardrail does not slow the project down, it keeps it standing.

The question is not how much time automating saves me. It is where I put the checkpoint, and what that checkpoint costs.

I paid to learn that. You can learn it first.
