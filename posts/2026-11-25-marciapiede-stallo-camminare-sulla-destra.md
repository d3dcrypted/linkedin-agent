We both step to the right, both stop, and look at each other.

It happens more than it should, and someone counted. In a hallway study at UT Austin, researchers logged 220 face-to-face approaches: people showed a clear bias for passing on each other's right, in a crowded corridor and in an empty one. The side is a habit, and the habit holds until somebody interrupts it.

The messy part is the signal. When the person walking at you looks where they actually intend to go, about one encounter in four ends in a conflict, meaning a brush, a blocked step or a full stop. When their gaze points somewhere else, it becomes closer to one in two. Same corridor, same pedestrians, twice the mess, and the only variable was a head pointing the wrong way.

Eye contact does not fix it. One experiment blocked a walker's gaze with mirrored sunglasses and the pairs still coordinated exactly as before. Hand one of them a phone to type on instead, and the coordination falls apart (iScience, 2022). Walkers read shoulders, pace, the small turn of the body, and they mostly read it before the other person has moved. Eye-tracking work shows the same thing from the other side: people look away from the gaze of whoever is coming and skirt past on the other side (Nummenmaa et al., 2009). Two people running that routine at the same moment is the dance.

Strip it down and it is a coordination game nobody signed up for. Any side works as long as we both pick the same one, and with no time to negotiate you need a default that both of us assume the other assumes. Schelling named it the focal point in 1960, and noted that how obvious it looks depends on the time, the place and the people in it.

So the practical version, from someone who loses this twice a week: pick a side early and make it visible. The hesitation is what parks you both in the middle. Blunt question, then: in your last standoff, did you commit, or did you wait to see what the other person would do?

The thought is human, the typing is a machine's. The machine has never had to pick a side.
